RGD Solutions · Landing page directions

Ten directions, one page of copy.

Every artboard on this canvas is the same landing page (same sections, same words) designed ten different ways, so the comparison is purely about palette, type, logo and mood. Pick one, or name a mix: the palette of one, the type of another, the layout of a third.

01
Obsidian Editorial
Near-black, bone white, burnt orange. Instrument Serif and Manrope. Luxury consultancy with huge type.
Tradeoff: dark pages read as premium but can feel cold for a one-person shop.
02
Swiss Precision
White, ink, one signal red. Archivo and Space Grotesk on a strict 12-column grid.
Tradeoff: very rational; relies on typography alone for warmth.
03
Terminal
Slate and phosphor amber. JetBrains Mono. The hero is a shell session.
Tradeoff: instantly credible to CTOs, less so to non-technical founders.
04
Warm Studio
Cream paper, terracotta, olive. DM Serif Display and DM Sans. Grain and organic shapes.
Tradeoff: the most human; the least "AI engineering" at first glance.
05
Electric
Deep indigo with a violet-to-cyan mesh. Syne and Manrope. Glow, glass, motion.
Tradeoff: high energy, but the most common look in AI startups right now.
06
Brutalist
Raw white, 3px black borders, highlighter yellow. Archivo Black and Space Mono. Marquee client strip.
Tradeoff: unforgettable and loud; some enterprise buyers will find it brash.
07
Aurora Glass
Navy with teal and rose aurora. Outfit and Manrope. Frosted-glass cards and depth.
Tradeoff: polished modern SaaS; glass effects need care on low-end devices.
08
Bento
Warm neutral, charcoal, one blue. Plus Jakarta Sans and Onest. Everything is a tile.
Tradeoff: product-like and scannable; less narrative flow down the page.
09
Retro Future
Mustard, rust, teal stripes. Bricolage Grotesque and Space Mono. Playful and memorable.
Tradeoff: the most personality; the least conventional for enterprise procurement.
10
Blueprint
Blueprint blue, white lineart, cyan grid. Chakra Petch and IBM Plex Sans. Drafting-table feel.
Tradeoff: a real story (structural engineering) and distinctive; the theme must stay subtle to avoid kitsch.
